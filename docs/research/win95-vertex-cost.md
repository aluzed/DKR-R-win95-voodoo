# What a vertex costs on the target

A measurement from
[E04-S03](../stories/E04-hle-f3ddkr/E04-S03-matrices-and-vertex-transform.md),
14 August 2026, on the test machine — Pentium II 400 MHz, Windows 95.

## The figure

```text
  measurement: 20000 vertices x 20 passes in 273 ms
  that is 0.682 us per vertex in x87 floating point
  that is 24322 vertices in a 16.6 ms frame
```

A complete transformation: model-view-projection matrix applied, perspective
division, scaling to the screen, written straight out in the backend's format —
`dkr_render_vertex`, which has `GrVertex`'s layout.

## What this figure allows one to say

A frame at 60 Hz leaves 16.6 ms. **24,322 vertices** fit into it, if one does
nothing but transform them.

That is the raw budget, and it must be read as such: the transformation is not
alone in the frame. There remain the display-list decoding, the clipping, the
emission to Glide, and the fill rate — which is the item E08 will deal with.

## What this figure does not say

**It does not decide between floating point and fixed point**, and the ticket
asked for the decision to be made on a measurement. Only x87 is measured here.

Writing a fixed-point variant in haste would give a misleading figure: 16.16
multiplication requires 64-bit intermediates, perspective division does not come
naturally to it, and an approximate implementation would measure its own
clumsiness rather than the technique. Better a missing figure than a false one.

And the decision blocks nothing for now: it depends on the **number of vertices
per frame in a real scene**, which only the game can give — hence the ROM. If DKR
emits two to four thousand vertices per frame, x87 costs 1.4 to 2.7 ms, that is
8 to 16 % of the budget: significant, not fatal. If the scene emits twenty
thousand, the question changes in nature.

**It is E08-S01 that will supply that number**, and that is when the fixed-point
measurement will be worth making.

## Protocol

Twenty passes over twenty thousand vertices, that is four hundred thousand
transformations. The multiple passes are not a stylistic precaution:
`GetTickCount` advances in steps of **9 ms** on this machine (E02-S03), and a
single-pass measurement would measure the clock's granularity rather than the
code.

The vertices are generated by a progression that spreads them over a wide range,
so that the perspective division does not always work on the same value — a
constant divisor would lend itself to a prediction the real case does not offer.

The measurement is **ignored on the host**, and saying so is the most important
thing in this document: a modern Ryzen tells one nothing about a 400 MHz Pentium
II, and a figure taken there would give false assurance.
